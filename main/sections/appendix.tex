\section{Multilayer Perceptrons instead of Convolutional Networks}\label{sec:app-ez-mlp}

The reference implementation of EfficientZero is aimed at solving tasks with visual input
and therefore makes use of convolutional neural networks~\cite{ye_mastering_2021}.
Its observation is a stack of four $96\times96$ Atari frames and its representation
network consists of convolutions, pooling stages, and residual blocks.

This thesis does not make use of any image data.
Observations are usually block hit counts as well as other heterogeneous data like current
seed input and environment step phase~(\cref{sec:mdp-states}).
In particular, we do not need to make use of translation invariance of convolutional networks
which is necessary for image inputs.
We therefore replace every convolution with a fully connected layer, and every convolutional
residual block with the \gls{mlp} block of \cref{eq:app-residual-block}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-residual-block}
  R_{d,r}(x) = x + W_2\,\mathrm{GELU}\!\left(W_1\,\mathrm{LN}(x)+b_1\right)+b_2,
  \qquad W_1\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d},\quad W_2\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r},
\end{equation}

a pre-norm bottleneck of width $r$ around a skip connection of width $d$.
The latent state becomes a vector of width $L$ instead of a channel-height-width tensor.
Following the depth of the reference, the next layers include five residual blocks.

\paragraph{Layer instead of batch normalization}
The reference uses batch normalization~\cite{ioffe_batch_2015} at momentum 0.1.
Batch statistics mix different inputs together in a way that is not helpful
for \gls{rl} due to the constantly shifting distributions and differing episodes.
We use layer normalization~\cite{ba_layer_2016} instead.

\paragraph{No latent renormalization}
The reference min-max rescales every latent to $[0,1]$ before it enters a search node.
The layer normalization at the head of every residual block already presents each linear layer with
a unit-scale input, so we leave out the reference min-max normalization.

\paragraph{Action encoding}
The reference appends the action to the latent as an additional channel plane, spending one of the
64 channels on it.
We concatenate a one-hot vector of width $|\mathcal{A}|$, so the dynamics network reads
$L+|\mathcal{A}|$ inputs and returns $L$.
The reference's identity skip connection is kept by adding back only the first $L$ components of that input.

\paragraph{Optimizer}
The reference trains with SGD at momentum 0.9, an initial learning rate of 0.2 under a warm-up and
a step decay, and a coupled L2 penalty of $10^{-4}$.
It provides a learning rate schedule that is dependent on the current gradient step.
Since we use an unbounded learner, we therefore train with AdamW~\cite{adamw} at a constant
learning rate of \num{1e-3} and decay $10^{-4}$.

\paragraph{Zero-initialized outputs}
The last linear layer of the policy, value and value-prefix heads is initialized to zero, as in the
reference.
An untrained policy head is then exactly uniform and an untrained value or value prefix decodes to
exactly $0$, so the first searches of a run are unbiased by initialization noise.

\section{Categorical Value and Value-Prefix Targets}\label{sec:app-ez-categorical}

To stabilize training, implicit gradient clipping is provided by modeling the value and value-prefix heads
as classification instead of regression problems.\index{categorical value target}
Each head emits a distribution over a fixed support~\cite{schrittwieser_mastering_2020,ye_mastering_2021}.
To convert a scalar target $y$ into this representation, it is first compressed by the invertible transform

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-scalar-transform}
  \chi(y)=\operatorname{sgn}(y)\left(\sqrt{|y|+1}-1\right)+\epsilon\,y,
  \qquad \epsilon=10^{-3},
\end{equation}

which is near-linear for small $|y|$ and square-root-like for large $|y|$,
and then two-hot encoded\index{two-hot encoding} onto the support $z_1<\dots<z_B$ of $B$ evenly spaced points.
Targets outside $[z_1,z_B]$ are clamped to the boundary.
A head is read back in the opposite order: its logits are softmaxed, the expectation under that
distribution is taken against the support, and the result is mapped back with

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-scalar-transform-inverse}
  \chi^{-1}(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\left[
    \left(\frac{\sqrt{1+4\epsilon\left(|x|+1+\epsilon\right)}-1}{2\epsilon}\right)^{2}-1
  \right].
\end{equation}

Both supports are a per-target setting, since $[z_1,z_B]$ has to cover the returns the reward scale
of a target actually produces.

\chapter{Detailed Loss Calculation}\label{app:loss}

\Cref{sec:ez-loss} gives the training objective in the form the EfficientZero paper states it.
We explain in detail how batches are assembled, which positions each
term of \cref{eq:ez-loss} is allowed to see, and the exact form of the four terms.

Throughout, $N$ is the batch size, $K$ the number of unroll steps, and a batch therefore covers
$(K+1)N$ supervised positions, indexed by the sample $b\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ and the unroll step
$k\in\{0,\dots,K\}$.
According to the EfficientZero configuration of \cref{tab:app-ez-params}, $N=128$ and $K=5$, so one gradient step
sees 768 positions.

\section{Sampled Batches}\label{sec:app-loss-batch}

A raw batch is created by the rollout worker when sampling positions from the replay buffer.
The reanalyze worker then re-runs \gls{mcts} with updated model parameters and receives
updated policy and value targets which it includes in the final batch.
It also updates the step priorities.
The training engine then unrolls the model and evaluates the loss.

\paragraph{Drawing the positions}
The rollout worker draws $N$ positions from the replay buffer with replacement, at a probability
proportional to their priority~\cite{schaul_prioritized_2016}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-priority}
  P(i)=\frac{p_i^{\alpha_{\mathrm{per}}}}{\sum_{j=1}^{M} p_j^{\alpha_{\mathrm{per}}}},
  \qquad \alpha_{\mathrm{per}}=0.6,
\end{equation}

where $p_i$ is the priority of position $i$ and $M$ the number of positions the buffer holds.
Drawing with replacement means a buffer holding fewer than $N$ episodes still yields a full
batch, which matters early in a run.
A position enters the buffer at the largest priority seen so far, so every collected transition
is trained on at least once before its priority becomes a measured quantity.

Sampling non-uniformly biases the gradient, which prioritized experience replay corrects with a
per-sample importance-sampling weight:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-is-weight}
  w_b=\frac{\left(M\,P(i_b)\right)^{-\beta_{\mathrm{per}}}}
           {\max_{b'}\left(M\,P(i_{b'})\right)^{-\beta_{\mathrm{per}}}},
  \qquad \beta_{\mathrm{per}}=0.4,
\end{equation}

normalized by the largest weight in the batch, so that $\max_b w_b=1$ and the correction only ever
scales a gradient down.

Each drawn position becomes the root of one training sample.
Sample $b$ starts at offset $t_b$ inside an episode $\tau_b$ of length $|\tau_b|$ and supervises
the $K+1$ positions $t_b,\dots,t_b+K$.
The last $K+n$ positions of a \emph{truncated} episode are held at priority zero and can never
be drawn, with $n$ the \gls{td} horizon of \cref{eq:ez-value-target}.
Episodes that end in a terminal keep every position afterwards sampleable and
everything past their end is an absorbing state.

\paragraph{Layout}
Every per-step field is stored step-major as $[K{+}1,N,\Cdot]$, so position $(b,k)$ occupies the
flat index $i=kN+b$.
This is the order in which the training engine concatenates the unrolled latents, which lets every
head evaluation and every loss reduction run once over all $(K+1)N$ rows.
\Cref{tab:app-loss-batch} lists what the rollout worker writes.

Observations at index $k$ are the ones that were used for choosing action $a_k$,
and not the ones that were produced by action $a_k$, so \code{observations} at
$k=0$ is exactly \code{initial\_observations}.
The bootstrap horizon $n_b$ is the age-scaled one of \cref{sec:ez-off-policy}: it is a property of
the sample rather than of the position, so it is constant along a sample's unroll.

\paragraph{What Reanalyze modifies}
The reanalyze worker runs one representation pass over the observation, bootstrap and initial rows
of the batch, then refreshes three quantities.

The policy targets are re-searched for the first $\lfloor\rho_r N\rfloor=126$ samples of every
depth block, at $\rho_r=0.99$, the remaining samples keep the visit counts stored at self-play
time.
Masked positions are zeroed after the search, because a search over a masked position's
all-zero observation still returns nonzero visit counts.
The surviving counts are normalized per row into the target distribution

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-policy-target}
  \pi^k_b(a)=\frac{N_{t_b+k}(a)}{\max\!\left(\sum_{a'}N_{t_b+k}(a'),\,10^{-8}\right)},
\end{equation}

where the floor in the denominator leaves a zeroed row as the zero vector instead of dividing by
zero.

The value targets are recomputed for every position of the batch as the $n$-step return

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-value-target}
  z^k_b=\underbrace{\sum_{i=0}^{n_b-1}\gamma^{i}\,r_{t_b+k+i+1}}_{\text{read at rollout time}}
        \;+\;\ind{t_b+k+n_b<|\tau_b|}\,\gamma^{n_b}\,
        \hat v\!\left(o_{t_b+k+n_b}\right),
\end{equation}

the value target of~\cref{eq:ez-value-target}.
The bootstrap $\hat v$ is the value head of the current weights, decoded to a scalar with
\cref{eq:app-scalar-transform-inverse}.
Reading the value head directly halves the reanalyze search cost as compared
to using the resulting \gls{mcts} root value, and the reference reports using the
\gls{mcts} root value to be only a marginal improvement.
Positions whose bootstrap falls past the end of the episode drop the bootstrap term, which
leaves the truncated reward sum as the target; at a position past the end of a terminal episode
that sum is empty and the target is exactly $0$.

The priorities are recomputed for the $N$ root positions, which are the ones that were drawn:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-priority-update}
  p_{i_b}=\left|\hat v(o_{t_b})-z^0_b\right|
          +c_r\max\!\left(z^0_b,0\right)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{per}},
  \qquad \epsilon_{\mathrm{per}}=10^{-6}.
\end{equation}

The first term is the value error of \cref{sec:ez-loop}.
The optional second term keeps high-return transitions in the training mix after the value head
has learned to predict them, at which point the error term alone would decay to zero and drop them.
It was used for preliminary testing only and is turned off for all experiments ($c_r=0$).

\section{Mask Calculation}\label{sec:app-loss-masks}

Two masks separate the positions a batch may supervise from the padding that fills the rest
due to mismatching episode lengths.
The in-range mask marks the positions that lie inside the sampled episode,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-mask}
  m^k_b=\ind{t_b+k<|\tau_b|},
\end{equation}

and the value mask additionally admits a bounded number of positions past the end:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-value-mask}
  m^{v,k}_b=\ind{t_b+k<|\tau_b|+d_a},\qquad d_a=5.
\end{equation}

Past the end of a terminal episode a position is an absorbing state, and the targets
\cref{eq:app-loss-value-target} already produces correct values of $0$ and a
value prefix that no longer grows.
Supervising them is what teaches the model that a terminal action pays nothing.
Without it, the value head is never evaluated at a post-terminal latent and its
extrapolation there is uncontrolled.
We found this to be essential to the sequence target (\cref{sec:target-sequence}).
The dynamics network unrolls across an absorbing position under a \emph{random} action rather than
under the padded action $0$, so that the signal covers the whole action space instead of one
action of it.
A truncated episode can never reach this case, since the buffer already excluded the positions
whose unroll would cross the cut.
The depth $d_a$ bounds how far the supervision reaches: on a target whose episodes are much
shorter than $K$, admitting every position past the end would fill the value and value-prefix
losses with exact-zero targets.

\paragraph{From masks to per-position weights}
The masks, the importance-sampling weight and the batch normalization are folded on the host into
one weight per position and loss term:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-weights}
  w^p_{b,k}=\frac{w_b}{N},
  \qquad
  w^v_{b,k}=m^{v,k}_b\,\frac{w_b}{N},
  \qquad
  w^c_{b,k}=m^{k}_b\,\ind{k>0}\,\frac{w_b}{N}.
\end{equation}

Each loss term is then a single weighted sum over the $(K+1)N$ rows, with no per-step masking or
reduction of its own.
Normalization is by the full batch size $N$: a batch of short episodes
contributes proportionally less gradient rather than being scaled back up.

The policy term carries no mask, because \cref{eq:app-loss-policy-target} leaves a masked row as
the zero vector and a zero target contributes zero to a cross entropy.
The value and value-prefix terms run on $m^{v,k}_b$ and therefore include the absorbing positions.
The consistency term runs on $m^k_b$ and is additionally zero at $k=0$, where the prediction $s^0$
and the target $h(o_{t_b})$ are the same latent and the term would carry no information about the
dynamics network.

\section{The Unroll}\label{sec:app-loss-unroll}

The training engine encodes the batch's initial observations once,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-unroll-root}
  s^0_b=h(o_{t_b};\theta),
\end{equation}

and then applies the dynamics network along the actions the batch recorded:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-unroll-step}
  s^k_b=\operatorname{sg}_{1/2}\!\left(g\!\left(\left[s^{k-1}_b,\ \mathrm{onehot}(a_{t_b+k})\right];\theta\right)\right),
  \qquad k=1,\dots,K,
\end{equation}

where $\operatorname{sg}_{1/2}(y)=\tfrac12 y+\tfrac12\operatorname{sg}(y)$ leaves the forward value
untouched and halves the gradient flowing back through each dynamics application, as in
MuZero~\cite{schrittwieser_mastering_2020}.
The value-prefix \gls{lstm} is stepped alongside the chain, consuming $s^k_b$ and emitting the
prefix logits from the hidden state it has just produced.

The unroll is the only sequential part of a gradient step.
Its $K+1$ latents are concatenated into one $[(K{+}1)N,L]$ tensor, and the policy, value,
projection and prediction networks are evaluated once over it.

\paragraph{Carry resets}
The \gls{lstm} carry is zeroed every $5$ steps, so that training runs the recurrence over exactly
the windows the search runs it over.
The value-prefix targets of \cref{sec:app-loss-vp} restart at the same boundary.

\section{The Four Loss Terms}\label{sec:app-loss-terms}

Every categorical term is the cross entropy

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-crossentropy}
  \ell(q,\hat{y})=-\sum_{j}q_j\,\log\operatorname{softmax}(\hat{y})_j
\end{equation}

between a target distribution $q$ and predicted logits $\hat{y}$.
$\phi_v$ and $\phi_x$ denote the encoding of a scalar into a two-hot distribution over the value
and value-prefix support of \cref{sec:app-ez-categorical}, that is $\chi$ of
\cref{eq:app-scalar-transform} followed by the two-hot encoding.

\subsection{Policy}\label{sec:app-loss-policy}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-policy}
  \mathcal{L}_p=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{b=0}^{N-1}w^p_{b,k}\;
  \ell\!\left(\pi^k_b,\ p(s^k_b;\theta)\right)
\end{equation}

The target $\pi^k_b$ is the normalized visit distribution of
\cref{eq:app-loss-policy-target} --- fresh for the re-searched samples, recorded at self-play time
for the rest.

\subsection{Value}\label{sec:app-loss-value}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-value}
  \mathcal{L}_v=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{b=0}^{N-1}w^v_{b,k}\;
  \ell\!\left(\phi_v\!\left(z^k_b\right),\ v(s^k_b;\theta)\right)
\end{equation}

The target $z^k_b$ is the $n$-step return of \cref{eq:app-loss-value-target}, whose reward sum was
read from the buffer at rollout time and whose bootstrap was evaluated under the current weights at
reanalyze time.

\subsection{Value Prefix}\label{sec:app-loss-vp}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-vp}
  \mathcal{L}_x=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{b=0}^{N-1}w^v_{b,k}\;
  \ell\!\left(\phi_x\!\left(x^k_b\right),\ \vpnet_k(s^{0:k}_b;\theta)\right),
  \qquad
  x^k_b=\sum_{j=k_0(k)}^{k-1}m^j_b\,r_{t_b+j+1},
\end{equation}

where $\vpnet_k$ is the logits the \gls{lstm} emits at step $k$ and $k_0(k)$ is the last carry
reset at or before $k$.
The prediction is therefore the only one in the objective that depends on the whole latent prefix
$s^0,\dots,s^k$ rather than on $s^k$ alone.

$x^k_b$ deviates from \cref{eq:ez-value-prefix} in two ways.

\paragraph{The sum is exclusive}
$x^k_b$ accumulates the rewards collected to \emph{reach} step $k$, so the root carries the empty
sum $x^0_b=0$ and the terminal reward of an episode enters a supervised target only at the first
absorbing position.

\paragraph{The sum is undiscounted}
The reference drops the $\gamma^{i}$ of \cref{eq:ez-value-prefix} on the grounds that the horizon
is short and $\gamma$ close to one, and we follow it.
Accumulation is gated by $m^j_b$ so that padded steps cannot introduce errors to the target.

\subsection{Consistency}\label{sec:app-loss-consistency}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-consistency}
  \mathcal{L}_c=-\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{b=0}^{N-1}w^c_{b,k}\;
  \operatorname{cossim}\!\Big(
    \prednet\big(\projnet(s^k_b;\theta);\theta\big),\;
    \operatorname{sg}\big(\projnet(h(o_{t_b+k};\theta);\theta)\big)
  \Big)
\end{equation}

The target branch runs the representation network a second time, over the \code{observations}
field of the whole batch rather than over the roots alone, and is detached
(\cref{sec:ez-consistency}).

\section{Total Loss and Gradient Step}\label{sec:app-loss-total}

The four terms are weighted and scaled by the number of dynamics applications:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-loss-total}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta)=\frac{1}{K}\left(
    \lambda_p\,\mathcal{L}_p+\lambda_v\,\mathcal{L}_v
    +\lambda_x\,\mathcal{L}_x+\lambda_c\,\mathcal{L}_c\right),
\end{equation}

with the coefficients of \cref{tab:app-ez-params}.
The scale is $1/K$ and not $1/(K+1)$: it counts the applications of the dynamics network, which is
one fewer than the number of supervised positions.

The gradient of \cref{eq:app-loss-total} is clipped to a norm of $5$ and applied with
AdamW~\cite{adamw} at weight decay $10^{-4}$.
The four term values and their weighted sum are kept on the device and read
back at the start of the following step.
