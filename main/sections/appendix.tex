\Cref{chap:efficientzero} names the seven networks by what they compute.
This appendix gives their bodies, the categorical encoding through which the scalar heads are
trained, and the weighted loss that \cref{eq:ez-loss} abbreviates.
Widths appear here as symbols; \cref{tab:app-ez-widths} resolves them.

\section{From Convolutions to Multilayer Perceptrons}\label{sec:app-ez-mlp}

The reference implementation of EfficientZero is convolutional throughout~\cite{ye_mastering_2021}.
Its observation is a stack of four $96\times96$ Atari frames, and its representation network is a
downsampling stem --- two strided $3\times3$ convolutions and two average-pooling stages,
interleaved with four residual blocks --- followed by one further residual block at 64 channels.
The latent state is therefore a $64\times6\times6$ tensor, and each head first reduces it with a
$1\times1$ convolution to 16 channels and flattens the result before a two-layer \gls{mlp}.

Neither observation space of this thesis is an image.
Their coverage part is a bucket array: the block hit tracker folds basic-block addresses into a
fixed number of buckets by hashing (\cref{sec:impl-coverage}), so two neighboring indices hold two
unrelated basic blocks.
The rest is heterogeneous --- seed bytes, a phase one-hot, the staging summary and the depth
record (\cref{sec:mdp-states}) --- concatenated into one flat vector.
A convolution buys weight sharing in exchange for locality and translation invariance, and hashing
destroys both assumptions: the units a kernel reads together are related only by their hash
values, and the pattern it learns at one offset means nothing at the next.
We therefore replace every convolution with a fully connected layer, and every convolutional
residual block with the \gls{mlp} block of \cref{eq:app-residual-block}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-residual-block}
  R_{d,r}(x) = x + W_2\,\mathrm{GELU}\!\left(W_1\,\mathrm{LN}(x)+b_1\right)+b_2,
  \qquad W_1\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d},\quad W_2\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r},
\end{equation}

a pre-norm bottleneck of width $r$ around a skip connection of width $d$.
The latent state becomes a vector of width $L$ instead of a channel-height-width tensor, which
also removes the heads' $1\times1$ reduction: what is left of a head is the \gls{mlp} that
followed it.
The trunk keeps the depth of the reference rather than its layout --- five residual blocks
against the stem's four plus one --- since the single linear layer that projects an observation to
the latent width does no mixing of its own.

The remaining departures from the reference architecture are consequences of the same change or of
the scale this thesis trains at.

\paragraph{Layer instead of batch normalization}
The reference normalizes with batch normalization~\cite{ioffe_batch_2015} at momentum 0.1.
Batch statistics tie the prediction for one input to the other inputs it happens to be batched
with, which \gls{mcts} cannot tolerate: the batch a node is evaluated in is a consequence of the
tree shape and of the pool of environments stepped in parallel (\cref{sec:impl-efficientzero}), and
evaluation runs a single environment at batch size one.
We normalize with layer normalization~\cite{ba_layer_2016} instead, so the value the search reads
back for a node is a function of that node alone.

\paragraph{No latent renormalization}
The reference min-max rescales every latent to $[0,1]$ before it enters a search node.
We do not reproduce this.
The layer normalization at the head of every residual block already presents each linear layer with
a unit-scale input, whereas a min-max rescaling is set by the two extreme units of the vector and
moves with any one of them.

\paragraph{Action encoding}
The reference appends the action to the latent as an additional channel plane, spending one of the
64 channels on it.
We concatenate a one-hot vector of width $|\mathcal{A}|$, so the dynamics network reads
$L+|\mathcal{A}|$ inputs and returns $L$; the reference's identity skip is kept by adding back only
the first $L$ components of that input.

\paragraph{Optimizer}
The reference trains with SGD at momentum 0.9, an initial learning rate of 0.2 under a warm-up and
a step decay, and a coupled L2 penalty of $10^{-4}$.
Both halves of that schedule are fractions of a fixed total of \num{120000} gradient steps, and
this implementation has no such total: its outer loop is unbounded, and the optional run bound may
be a count of learner iterations, of trajectories, of environment steps or of wall-clock time, only
the first of which fixes a gradient-step count in advance.
We therefore train with AdamW at a constant \num{1e-3} with the same $10^{-4}$ decay applied
decoupled, and clip gradients at norm 5 per parameter tensor rather than globally.

\paragraph{Zero-initialized outputs}
The last linear layer of the policy, value and value-prefix heads is initialized to zero, as in the
reference.
An untrained policy head is then exactly uniform and an untrained value or value prefix decodes to
exactly $0$, so the first searches of a run are unbiased by initialization noise.

The value-prefix network is the only recurrent one and the only one whose forward pass is not a
function of its input alone.
It is stepped once per unroll step, consumes the latent of that step and emits the prefix logits
from the hidden state it just produced, and its carry is zeroed every five steps
(\cref{sec:app-ez-objective}).
The projection head closes without an activation, which is what makes its output an embedding to
compare rather than a representation to read.

The ladder halves every width from one rung to the next with one exception: the value-prefix
\gls{lstm} drops by a factor of four between \emph{Medium} and \emph{Small}.
Because its four gates cost $4\,(L w_\ell + w_\ell^2 + 2 w_\ell)$ parameters and are therefore
quadratic in $w_\ell$, that one deviation carries most of the difference between the two rungs:
the value-prefix network falls from \num{421125} to \num{40581} parameters while the whole model
falls from \num{1877770} to \num{704010}.

A rung does not cost the same on both corpus targets.
The representation network's input layer alone holds $|o|\cdot L$ weights, which at
\emph{Medium} is \SI{56}{\percent} of cJSON's parameters and \SI{84}{\percent} of libxml2's ---
libxml2 observes four times as many coverage buckets (\cref{sec:target-libxml2}).
Capacity added by a rung therefore lands mostly in the encoder on the wider target and is spread
more evenly on the narrower one, so the two are not the same experiment run at two scales.

\section{Categorical Value and Value-Prefix Targets}\label{sec:app-ez-categorical}

The value and value-prefix heads emit a distribution over a fixed support rather than a scalar,
which trades the unbounded gradients of a regression loss for the bounded ones of a cross
entropy~\cite{schrittwieser_mastering_2020,ye_mastering_2021}.
A scalar target $y$ is first compressed by the invertible transform

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-scalar-transform}
  \chi(y)=\operatorname{sgn}(y)\left(\sqrt{|y|+1}-1\right)+\epsilon\,y,
  \qquad \epsilon=10^{-3},
\end{equation}

which is near-linear for small $|y|$ and square-root-like for large
$|y|$\todo{cite: value-scaling transform (Pohlen et al.\ 2018) --- not in references/*.bib yet},
and then two-hot encoded onto the support $z_1<\dots<z_B$ of $B$ evenly spaced points:
the two neighbors bracketing $\chi(y)$ receive the mass $1-m$ and $m$, where $m$ is the fractional
position of $\chi(y)$ between them.
A target outside $[z_1,z_B]$ is clamped to the boundary, and a target that is not a number is
encoded as $0$ so that it contributes no signal instead of poisoning the batch.

A head is read back in the opposite order: its logits are softmaxed, the expectation under that
distribution is taken against the support, and the result is mapped back with

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-scalar-transform-inverse}
  \chi^{-1}(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\left[
    \left(\frac{\sqrt{1+4\epsilon\left(|x|+1+\epsilon\right)}-1}{2\epsilon}\right)^{2}-1
  \right].
\end{equation}

Both supports are a per-target setting, since $[z_1,z_B]$ has to cover the returns the reward scale
of a target actually produces, and $B$ enters the parameter count as the output width of both
heads.

\section{The Weighted Loss}\label{sec:app-ez-objective}

\Cref{eq:ez-loss} omits three things the implementation cannot: which positions of an unroll are
supervised at all, how a batch is normalized, and how gradients are scaled along the unroll.
Writing $\mathcal{B}$ for the batch, $w_b$ for the importance-sampling weight of sample $b$
(\cref{sec:app-ez-priorities}) and $m^{\text{v}}_{b,k},m_{b,k}\in\{0,1\}$ for the two masks below,
the loss actually minimized is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-ez-loss-full}
  \begin{split}
    \mathcal{L}(\theta)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{w_b}{|\mathcal{B}|}\Big[
      &\lambda_p\,\ell^{\,p}_{b,k}
      + m^{\text{v}}_{b,k}\left(\lambda_v\,\ell^{\,v}_{b,k}+\lambda_x\,\ell^{\,x}_{b,k}\right)\\
      &+ m_{b,k}\,\mathbf{1}[k>0]\;\lambda_c\,\ell^{\,c}_{b,k}\Big].
  \end{split}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Masks}
A position sampled near the end of a trajectory has fewer than $K$ successors, and the unroll runs
past the end of the data.
$m_{b,k}$ marks the positions that are backed by a real transition.
The policy term needs no mask because its targets are zero-padded, which makes the cross entropy of
a padded position exactly zero.
The value and value-prefix terms use $m^{\text{v}}_{b,k}$, which additionally covers a bounded
number of absorbing positions past the end of a trajectory --- value target $0$, value prefix
frozen at the episode return.
Without them a terminal transition's outcome is never trained on, and the value at post-terminal
latents is left to extrapolation that the search will happily mine.
The consistency term is skipped at $k=0$, where no dynamics step has run yet and the two branches
of \cref{eq:ez-consistency} would compare a latent with itself.

\paragraph{Normalization}
Each term is summed over positions and divided by the full batch size, not by the number of
positions that survived its mask.
An unroll step at which few samples are valid therefore contributes proportionally less, rather
than being upweighted to the same total as a fully valid one.

\paragraph{Gradient scaling}
The total is divided by $K$, the number of dynamics applications rather than the number of
supervised positions, so that the gradient reaching a shared parameter does not grow with the
unroll length.
Each dynamics output additionally passes through $x\mapsto\tfrac12 x+\tfrac12\operatorname{sg}(x)$,
which leaves the forward pass unchanged and halves the gradient flowing back through that step.
The latent at unroll step $k$ therefore contributes to the representation network with weight
$2^{-k}$, which keeps the encoder from being dominated by the deepest, least reliable end of the
unroll.
The consistency loss contributes a stop-gradient of its own: its target branch
$\projnet(h(o_{t+k};\theta);\theta)$ is detached, so the representation network is not trained to
meet the dynamics network halfway.

\paragraph{The recurrent window}
The value-prefix \gls{lstm} carries its hidden state across the unroll and is reset every five
steps, in training and in search alike.
Its target is reset at the same boundary and is the \emph{exclusive} prefix
$\sum_{j<k}\gamma^{j}u_{t+j+1}$, so the root's target is the empty sum $0$ and the difference of a
child's and its parent's prefix telescopes to the reward on the edge between them
(\cref{sec:ez-value-prefix}).

\section{Replay Priorities}\label{sec:app-ez-priorities}

The value loss of \cref{eq:ez-loss} is what \gls{per}~\cite{schaul_prioritized_2016} prioritizes
on.
The reanalyze worker already computes a fresh value target $z_b$ for every sampled position and
already holds the head's prediction $v(s^0_b;\theta)$, so it writes back

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-ez-priority}
  p_b=\left|v(s^0_b;\theta)-z_b\right|+10^{-6}
\end{equation}

as that position's priority, the constant being a floor that keeps a perfectly predicted position
samplable.
A position is then sampled with probability proportional to $p_b^{\alpha}$ and its contribution to
\cref{eq:app-ez-loss-full} is corrected by the importance-sampling weight
$w_b\propto(N p_b^{\alpha}/\sum_j p_j^{\alpha})^{-\beta}$, for the two exponents of
\cref{tab:app-ez-params} and over the $N$ samplable positions in the buffer.
The weights are divided by the largest one in the batch, so the largest is exactly $1$ and the
correction only ever scales a sample down.
A freshly inserted trajectory has no measured error yet and enters at the largest priority observed
so far, which puts it ahead of older positions whose error has already been measured and found
small; the reanalyze worker overwrites that placeholder with \cref{eq:app-ez-priority} the first
time the position is drawn.
