\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:discussion-threats}

There are multiple possible failure modes to this thesis leading to invalid or lackluster conclusions.

\paragraph{No comparison to conventional fuzzers is included.}
We do not compare our approach to any conventional fuzzers like AFL++.
Results can therefore only be interpreted as showing the general feasibility of our approach,
without quantifying potential advantages or drawbacks as compared to traditional \gls{cgf}.
We leave this open as future work.

\paragraph{The return might climb due to a learned value function, and not due to planning.}
The core part of EfficientZero is \gls{mcts}-based planning.
We do not include a parameter study with only a single \gls{mcts} iteration.
This is necessary to determine whether planning is useful at all, or whether the
agent only learns a value function.
It is important to quantify how many planning iterations should be used exactly since wall-clock
execution time scales with the number of simulations.

\paragraph{Four seeds are not enough.}
The spread between seeds for various arms is larger than the spread between the arms
itself.
Only five of the thirteen trained arms besides the baseline have a
seed range disjoint from the baseline's \num{613} to \num{668}:
\code{ALPHA004}, \code{NOCOV}, \code{REUSE4}, \code{T15} and
\code{T25}~(\cref{tab:campaign-inventory}).
The ablation study should be re-run with more seeds to sharpen the results.

\paragraph{Zeroing the coverage observation beats the baseline.}

\code{NOCOV} zeroes the coverage bitmap in the
observation~(\cref{tab:cjson-arms}) and ends at \num{755} blocks (\num{678} to
\num{799}) against the baseline's
\num{645} (\num{613} to \num{668}).
An agent that cannot see the coverage beats the baseline, indicating that
coverage information might not play an important part, at least in this target.
Interestingly, \code{NOSEED}, which only has access to the coverage and not the seed,
still manages to advance.
This indicates that either coverage or the seed itself is already enough for learning.
We expect the usefulness of coverage as an observation to be strongly
target-dependent, but have not tested this.

\paragraph{Wall-clock figures are measured under contention.}
For a given campaign, we run multiple evaluations in parallel as described in~\cref{sec:methodology-setup}.
Since these evaluations share the GPU, the actual wall-clock figures are likely to be
pessimistic.
Measured on the baseline arm, one learner iteration takes about \SI{300}{\second} under the
campaign's concurrency versus \SI{96}{\second} with the GPU to itself.
The execution rates between \num{21} and \num{23} inputs per second and the three-orders-of-magnitude
gap to a native harness in \cref{sec:discussion-interpretation} are therefore contended figures that
overstate the gap by roughly that factor of three.

\paragraph{The corpus formulation is evaluated with a single fixed-length seed.}
Instead of a full corpus, we only make use of a single seed.
While this can serve as a preliminary test of whether the approach can work, it
is not sufficient to make any statements about how a full corpus with a larger action space might behave
in the end.
The selection decision is not exercised under a single seed, and an evaluation run
cannot follow multiple promising seeds at once.
