\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:discussion-threats}

Each threat below is stated in the form an examiner would put it, then what the design does about
it, then what remains.
The first is not mitigated at all, and it is the reason this thesis claims feasibility rather than
competitiveness.

\paragraph{No conventional fuzzer was run.}
The experiment that would settle whether any of this is worth doing --- the same targets, the same
wall-clock, AFL++ on one side and the agent on the other --- was never run, and no such measurement
exists anywhere in the recorded campaigns.
Every comparison in this thesis is therefore internal: the agent against a structure-blind random
control that shares its emulator, its harness and its execution budget~(\cref{sec:target-cjson}),
and configurations of the agent against each other.
Nothing mitigates this.
It bounds the strongest available claim to the one made in
\cref{sec:discussion-interpretation} --- that the learned policy beats undirected mutation per
execution --- and it makes every statement about competitiveness in this thesis an argument from
throughput arithmetic rather than a measurement.
A reader who wants the bake-off should treat the numbers here as the agent-side input to it and
nothing more.

\paragraph{The clearest results come from rewards that were designed for them.}
high-and-low and sequence are solved under rewards built to be solvable, including the coverage
sled that makes the final byte of the passcode learnable at all~(\cref{sec:target-sequence}), and a
result obtained under a designed reward says nothing about a target whose reward cannot be shaped.
The mitigation is that no real-world weight rests on those two targets: they are described as
feasibility tests in \cref{sec:methodology-setup}, the corpus-action targets run on raw coverage
with no shaping, and the configuration tuned on cJSON was transferred to libxml2 and picohttpparser
rather than re-tuned per target.
Two residuals qualify that transfer.
The transferred configuration is not literally the cJSON baseline --- it runs at $T=2$, which was
not one of the ablated temperatures, for twice as many iterations --- so "unchanged" describes the
intent and not the runs.
And the seeds of both transfer campaigns were not all produced by the same revision
(\cref{tab:campaign-inventory}), which makes them four runs of a configuration rather than four
repeated samples of one.

\paragraph{The win may be the learned value function rather than the planning.}
On a deterministic target a model-based planner and a well-fit value function over coverage are
hard to tell apart, and the contribution claimed here is the planner.
The ablation that separates them is a one-simulation arm, which was not run.
What was run is 50, 100 and 200 simulations, and coverage does not respond to any of it while
wall-clock scales with all of it~(\cref{sec:discussion-interpretation}); the divergence between the
policy prior and the search posterior also falls threefold over
training~(\cref{fig:policy-improvement}).
Both observations are consistent with the search mattering early and the network absorbing its
benefit later, and neither rules out the stronger reading that search width beyond 50 simulations
is simply wasted.
This thesis therefore makes no claim that planning as such is what produces its results.

\paragraph{Coverage is a proxy for bugs, and the coverage measured here is itself approximate.}
Every objective, reward and headline number in this thesis is basic-block coverage; no claim about
bugs found is made beyond what the oracle of \cref{sec:fuzzing-definitions} detected.
The proxy is standard in greybox fuzzing and it is what makes the comparison to a coverage-guided
baseline meaningful at all, so the choice is deliberate rather than incidental.
Two residuals remain.
Coverage is collected at block rather than edge granularity and is hashed into a fixed bucket array,
so distinct blocks collide and "new coverage" is an approximation whose error grows with the size
of the target~(\cref{sec:impl-coverage}).
And a policy optimised against a proxy will exploit the proxy: nothing in the reward distinguishes a
deeply nested input that reaches new blocks from one that reaches new blocks and would also expose a
defect.

The two threats an examiner will put together are the first and the third: a planner was used to
climb a reward on targets chosen for it, and neither the value of the planning nor the value of the
whole approach was measured against the technique it means to improve on.
The answer this thesis can give is that the learned policy beats undirected mutation by a factor of
\num{71} per execution on cJSON under a raw-coverage reward, that it discovers nesting on two
recursive-descent parsers without being told the grammar, and that both facts are worth having
precisely because they are the preconditions for the comparison that was not run.
What it cannot give is that comparison.
