\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:discussion-threats}

There are multiple possible failure modes to this thesis leading to invalid or lackluster conclusions.

\paragraph{No comparison to conventional fuzzers is included.}
We do not compare our approach to any conventional fuzzers like AFL++.
Results can therefore only be interpreted as showing the general feasibility of our approach,
without quantifying potential advantages or drawbacks as compared to traditional \gls{cgf}.
We leave this open as future work.

\paragraph{The return might climb due to a learned value function, and not due to planning.}
The core part of EfficientZero is \gls{mcts}-based planning.
We do not include a parameter study with only a single \gls{mcts} iteration.
This is necessary to determine whether planning is useful at all, or whether the
agent only learns a value function.
It is important to quantify how many planning iterations should be used exactly since wall-clock
execution time scales with the number of iteration.

\paragraph{Coverage stands in for bug finding, and no bug is searched for.}
The reward, the primary metric and every result of \cref{chap:results} are coverage.
Nothing in the pipeline detects, records or triages a faulting input, so an execution that corrupts
memory scores like any other execution that reaches new blocks.
All three corpus targets are mature libraries under continuous fuzzing, where a new bug within our
budget is unlikely in any case.
Whether the coverage the agent gains converts into bugs is therefore untested here.

\paragraph{Reported coverage counts buckets, not blocks.}
The block hit tracker folds block addresses into \num{4096} buckets on cJSON and picohttpparser and
\num{16384} on libxml2, and two blocks that collide are indistinguishable in the observation, in the
reward and in the reported coverage~(\cref{sec:impl-coverage}).
Assuming addresses hash uniformly, the \num{958} buckets of the best cJSON arm correspond to about
\num{1090} distinct blocks and libxml2's \num{5281} to about \num{6380}, an undercount of
\SI{12}{\percent} and \SI{17}{\percent}.
The compression grows with the coverage reached, so the distance between the best and the worst arm
is larger than the reported numbers suggest and every coverage figure in this thesis is a lower
bound on a saturating scale.
The agent optimizes the same aliased signal, so part of the reward it collects is a collision
artefact rather than new behavior of the \gls{put}.

\paragraph{The structural byte rate measures byte class, not well-formedness.}
It is the fraction of mutation-value actions that play a byte from a hand-picked, target-specific
set, 22 of 256 bytes for cJSON, and says nothing about where that byte lands or whether the seed
parses.
picohttpparser reaches a peak rate of \num{0.732}~(\cref{sec:results-picohttpparser}) while its best
inputs show no recognizable request structure at all~(\cref{tab:best-inputs}), and the best cJSON
seed \code{[[[[[2,[[[[[1\ldots}\ is unbalanced and never yields a document the parser accepts.
The metric supports the claim that the agent learns which bytes the parser reacts to, not the
stronger claim that it learns the grammar.

\paragraph{The two summary metrics of the cJSON campaign do not agree on the ranking.}
End-of-training coverage puts \code{T25} first at \num{958} blocks and \code{SIMS100} last among the
trained arms at \num{640}~(\cref{tab:campaign-inventory}), while the deep-seed rate puts
\code{REUSE4} first at \SI{87.46}{\percent} and \code{SIMS50} last at \SI{68.68}{\percent}, with
\code{W0} second at \SI{81.63}{\percent} from the second-worst coverage in the
campaign~(\cref{tab:deep-seed}).
The conclusion that exploration dominates rests on the first metric; under the second, \code{T25}
leads the baseline by \num{3.55} points, less than the seed spread of either arm.
Both are defensible summaries of the same runs and we have no principled argument for preferring
one, so which factor a reader takes to matter is decided by which metric is read.

\paragraph{Four seeds cannot separate arms this close.}
The within-arm spread of the deep-seed rate reaches \num{30.0} points on \code{SIMS50}
(\SI{56.16}{\percent} to \SI{86.14}{\percent}), \num{26.9} on \code{EDGE} and \num{22.2} on
\code{W0}~(\cref{tab:deep-seed}), larger than the distance between most pairs of arms.
On coverage, only five of the thirteen trained arms besides the baseline have a seed range disjoint
from the baseline's \num{613} to \num{668}: \code{ALPHA004}, \code{NOCOV}, \code{REUSE4},
\code{T15} and \code{T25}~(\cref{tab:campaign-inventory}).
We report medians and ranges and no significance test, and at $n=4$ no test would separate the
remaining arms.
Every ordering in \cref{sec:results-cjson} outside those five arms is compatible with run-to-run
variance.

\paragraph{The campaign ran in two waves on two revisions of the fuzzer.}
\code{BASE}, \code{CTRL-RAND}, \code{NOCOV}, \code{REUSE4}, \code{SIMS50}, \code{SIMS100},
\code{T15}, \code{T25} and \code{W0} ran between 29 July and 1 August 2026 on one revision, and
\code{ALPHA004}, \code{ALPHA010}, \code{EDGE}, \code{NOSEED} and \code{SMALL} between 2 and 4 August
on a later one that added the observation-hiding switches, the edge granularity and the per-episode
best-mutant probe.
That later revision also carries \code{MEDIUM}, a replication of the baseline under an identical
learner configuration and the same four PRG seeds, and it reaches \num{680} blocks against the
baseline's \num{645}.\todo{\code{MEDIUM} is labelled \code{FULL} in \cref{tab:campaign-inventory}
and \cref{sec:results-cjson}; no arm above the baseline width was ever run.}
Whether that gap comes from the revision or from nondeterminism between identically seeded runs, it
is larger than the baseline's distance to \code{W0}, \code{SIMS100} or \code{NOSEED} and bounds what
a difference of that size can mean.
Measured against the replication that shares their revision rather than against the baseline, the
effects of \code{SMALL} (\num{683}) and \code{NOSEED} (\num{669}) disappear.

\paragraph{Two arms barely perturb the factor they name.}
Under the level-based coverage score of \cref{sec:mdp-rewards}, an edge map and a block map differ by
about \SI{1}{\percent} per execution on a recursive-descent parser: a loop trips the same number of
times whichever map counts it, and most blocks have one dominant predecessor.
\code{EDGE} therefore compares a reward against a nearly identical one and does not test edge
coverage as a different signal.
The arm reported as the large model ran at the baseline's own hidden width, so the campaign varies
capacity downwards only and holds no evidence about the larger model that
\cref{sec:discussion-interpretation} expects to pay off on a richer target.

\paragraph{Blinding the observation does not cost coverage.}
\code{NOCOV} zeroes both coverage bitmaps in the observation~(\cref{tab:cjson-arms}), shares the
baseline's revision, and ends at \num{755} blocks (\num{678} to \num{799}) against the baseline's
\num{645} (\num{613} to \num{668}), a disjoint range~(\cref{tab:campaign-inventory}).
An agent that cannot see the coverage it has reached still improves coverage, which is evidence that
on cJSON the observation design of \cref{sec:mdp-states} carries little of the result and
the reward and exploration parameters carry it instead.
The same observation is used on the other two targets, where no arm tests it at all.

\paragraph{libxml2 and picohttpparser run a configuration that no arm evaluated.}
Both campaigns use a constant temperature of $T=2$ with a root concentration of
$\xi=\num{0.03906}$~(\cref{tab:campaign-inventory}), while the cJSON arms vary one factor at a time:
$T=1.5$ or $T=2.5$ at $\xi=1$, and $\xi\approx\num{0.04}$ under the default temperature schedule.
The transferred setting sits between two arms instead of reproducing one, and no interaction between
temperature and root noise is measured anywhere.
Its tuning basis is a single 16-byte JSON target, and neither transfer campaign carries a control
arm, so nothing tests whether the transfer succeeded.

\paragraph{The random control is matched in executions, not in compute.}
\code{CTRL-RAND} runs the baseline's execution budget in \SI{11.3}{\hour} against
\SI{21.3}{\hour}~(\cref{tab:campaign-inventory}), so at an equal wall-clock budget it would execute
roughly twice as many inputs.
The factor of \num{71} in \cref{sec:discussion-interpretation} is a per-execution statement, which is
the favorable framing for a method whose cost is time.
The control also exists on cJSON alone, so nothing separates the libxml2 and picohttpparser coverage
curves from what undirected mutation would have reached on those targets.

\paragraph{Wall-clock figures are measured under contention.}
The hours of \cref{tab:campaign-inventory} come from runs that share one GPU with the other runs of
their campaign~(\cref{sec:methodology-setup}).
Measured on the baseline arm, one learner iteration takes about \SI{300}{\second} under the
campaign's concurrency against \SI{96}{\second} with the GPU to itself.
The execution rates between \num{21} and \num{23} inputs per second and the three-orders-of-magnitude
gap to a native harness in \cref{sec:discussion-interpretation} are therefore contended figures that
overstate the gap by roughly that factor of three.
Wall-clock comparisons between arms hold only where concurrency was equal, which the campaign's
rolling schedule does not guarantee.

\paragraph{Every number is read at a fixed budget and from the final policy.}
Coverage in \cref{tab:campaign-inventory} is the median over the 128 parallel environments at the end
of training, not the best seed a run produced, and the structural byte rate ends below its peak on
all three targets~(\cref{fig:structural-decay}).
An arm is therefore scored partly on where in its decay it happened to be stopped, and a run that
peaked early and degraded is indistinguishable from one that never reached that level.
The budgets are 200 learner iterations on cJSON and 400 on the other two targets, one to two days per
run, against the days to weeks a \gls{cgf} campaign is given, so no ordering reported here
extrapolates to a budget an order of magnitude larger.

\paragraph{The corpus formulation is evaluated with a single fixed-length seed.}
Every corpus-action run holds one seed of 16 or 32 bytes~(\cref{sec:impl-corpus}), so the selection
and retention decisions that make the formulation a corpus \gls{mdp} are never exercised, and the
coverage a run can reach is bounded by what fits into that record.
picohttpparser's ceiling of \num{81} blocks is as consistent with a 32-byte request being too short
to reach the parser's interesting paths as with the target being unrewarding, and we do not separate
the two~(\cref{sec:results-picohttpparser}).
The targets are further emulated, parse a fixed-length buffer in a loop and are built for
determinism, libxml2 without threads and with several features disabled~(\cref{sec:target-libxml2}),
so the results describe a deterministic, length-fixed variant of each library rather than the
configuration a conventional fuzzer would attack.
