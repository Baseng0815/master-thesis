\subsection{cJSON}\label{sec:results-cjson}

cJSON is the only target we run together with a random control.
The trained agent beats the random control decisively.
Starting from a bootstrap corpus covering \num{240} blocks, the random control reaches a
median of \num{542} covered blocks and the frozen baseline \num{645}, while the best-performing
arm reaches \num{958}~(\cref{fig:ablation-strip}).

\input{figures/generated/ablation-strip}

The primary metric for this campaign measures the fraction of episodes whose
best seed reaches the 99th percentile of the random control and is provided in~\cref{tab:deep-seed}.
The baseline sits at \SI{76.66}{\percent} while the random control sits at \SI{1.08}{\percent}, about 71 times less.
Across the fourteen trained arms, the same metric spans \SI{68.68}{\percent} to
\SI{87.46}{\percent}.

\input{figures/generated/tab-deep-seed}
\input{figures/generated/return}

The two constant-temperature arms are the two best in the campaign, \code{T25} at \num{958} blocks
and \code{T15} at \num{936} against the scheduled baseline's \num{645}, and scaling the root
Dirichlet concentration to the action space adds a smaller amount, \code{ALPHA004} reaching
\num{876}.
We conclude that sufficient exploration is crucial for fuzzing performance, and that the
default temperature schedule provided by EfficientZero~\cite{ye_mastering_2021} is
not appropriate in this setting.

Halving and quartering the number of \gls{mcts} simulations changes coverage by less than the
seed spread within an arm: \num{687} blocks at 50 simulations, \num{640} at 100 and \num{645}
at 200.
Wall-clock time scales close to linearly with the number of \gls{mcts} simulations.
This shows that planning at 100 or even 50 steps does not decrease performance
compared to the baseline of 200 steps.
It is an open question whether this is a general limit of planning-based fuzzing,
or an artefact of the PUTs being too small.

Disabling the depth-record reward term leaves \code{W0} at \num{641} blocks, and it is the arm
whose structural byte rate decays furthest, ending at \num{0.214}.

The models of size \code{FULL} as well as \code{SMALL} perform better than the baseline, albeit
not significantly.
We expect a larger PUT with richer structure to benefit more from a large model.
\code{EDGE} coverage also performs slightly better than the baseline block coverage.

The highest-coverage input any cJSON run produced covers \num{831}
blocks~(\cref{tab:best-inputs}).
It is \code{[[[[[2,[[[[[1\ldots}, which is nesting the agent was never told about.
All four PRG seeds arrive at the same shape --- a run of opening brackets, a digit, a comma and
more brackets --- and they do so from three different arms, so the motif is a property of the
target and the reward rather than of one lucky run.

\subsection{libxml2}\label{sec:results-libxml2}

We run a single four-seed arm for libxml2 for 400 iterations and \num{3328128} guest executions in \SI{42.1}{\hour}.
Coverage rises from a bootstrap level of \num{1416} blocks to a median of \num{5281}, spanning
\num{5085} to \num{5419} across seeds.
The structural byte rate rises from a chance level of \num{0.054} to a peak of \num{0.725} at
\SI{31}{\percent} of the budget.
The highest-coverage input nests tags to a depth comparable with the cJSON result, at \num{4382}
blocks.

\subsection{picohttpparser}\label{sec:results-picohttpparser}

picohttpparser manages to improve its starting coverage from \num{26} to a
final median coverage across seeds of \num{81} in \SI{40.7}{\hour}.
The seed-to-seed range is a single block, \num{80} to \num{81}.
Coverage reaches \SI{90}{\percent} of its final level within the first quarter of the budget and
adds nothing in the final quarter~(\cref{fig:saturation}).

The structural byte rate rises from a chance level of \num{0.030} to a peak of
\num{0.732}, the same magnitude of rise as on the other two targets, while
peaking earlier at \SI{11}{\percent} of the budget.

\subsection{Structure, Collapse and Degradation}\label{sec:results-degradation}

The structural byte rate peaks before the end of training on all three targets and finishes below
its peak on each~(\cref{fig:structural-decay}).
It runs from \num{0.088} through a peak of \num{0.904} to \num{0.801} on cJSON, from \num{0.054}
through \num{0.725} to \num{0.548} on libxml2, and from \num{0.030} through \num{0.732} to
\num{0.358} on picohttpparser.
The peak arrives at \SI{24}{\percent}, \SI{31}{\percent} and \SI{11}{\percent} of the respective
budgets, so on every target the agent spends most of its run below the structural rate it had
already reached.

Over the same period, the policy tends to narrow onto fewer actions.
Per-position \gls{mcts} visit entropy falls from about \num{2.4} nats to between \num{0.08} and
\num{0.68} on cJSON, from \num{2.7} to between \num{0.37}, and \num{0.73} on libxml2 and from
\num{2.1} to between \num{0.41} and \num{0.55} on picohttpparser, and the positions narrow in
order, earliest first~(\cref{fig:collapse-entropy}).
The share of self-play plays taken by the single most-played action at a position rises
correspondingly~(\cref{fig:depth-actions}).

\input{figures/generated/collapse-entropy}
\input{figures/generated/depth-actions}

Search contributes less as training proceeds.
The mean divergence between the network's policy prior and the \gls{mcts} visit distribution at
the root falls from \num{0.99} to \num{0.28} nats on cJSON, from \num{4.16} to \num{0.59} on
libxml2 and from \num{4.16} to \num{0.44} on picohttpparser~(\cref{fig:policy-improvement}).
On cJSON the prior's own entropy falls from \num{5.55} to \num{0.79} nats over the same run, so
the prior search is correcting is itself an increasingly narrow one.

\input{figures/generated/policy-improvement}

Reward starvation does not accompany any of this.
Novelty is scored against the corpus union of the current episode, which is re-bootstrapped at
every reset, so a block found in one episode pays again in the next and the signal does not thin
out as run-level coverage saturates.
The share of replay-buffer transitions carrying a non-zero reward rises over training on all three
targets, from \num{0.012} to \num{0.147} on cJSON and from \num{0.004} to \num{0.104} on
picohttpparser~(\cref{fig:buffer-reward-density}).
libxml2 is the only target that turns over at all, peaking at \num{0.201} at iteration 327 and
ending at \num{0.188}.
